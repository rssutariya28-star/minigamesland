Privacy Policy

For the iOS app 1v1 Battle, formerly Big Battle

The short version: there is no account and no sign-up. To match you with a rival and run a live battle, the game sends your nickname, avatar and region to its game server, and your scores to your rival while you play. When you win, your nickname, champion, region and number of wins appear on the daily and weekly leaderboard. The app also uses Google's Firebase for anonymous usage statistics, to tell you when an update is out, and for the occasional message on the home screen. From version 1.3 the game is paid for by ads from Google AdMob, shown between battles and along the bottom of some screens, never during a battle; they are only chosen using your activity in other apps if you allow it when your iPhone asks. Nothing identifies you by name, and matchmaking records are deleted within a day.

No account needed

You never give us an email address, phone number, password or real name. When you first look for a battle, the app quietly creates an anonymous guest account on the game server: a random ID, with a sign-in token kept on your device. It is not linked to your Apple ID or to any other account.

What stays on your device

Your nickname, your chosen hero, the guest sign-in token, your level and XP, how many battles you've played and won, and any names you've reported are saved on your device so they are there next time. Uninstalling the app removes them.

What the game server receives, and why

1v1 Battle uses Supabase as its game server, hosted in Mumbai, India. It receives only what matchmaking and live battles need:

What Firebase receives, and why

Three Google Firebase services are built into the app. None of them receives your nickname, your scores or anything you type.

ServiceWhat it is forWhat it may receive
Firebase Analytics Counts anonymous, aggregated usage, such as how many people open the app each day and how long they play, so we can see what to improve. It also tells Google Ads when the app is opened for the first time, so we can see which of our own ads for 1v1 Battle bring new players. An app instance identifier, basic device and app details (model, OS version, language, app version) and a rough country or region. It is not linked to you. If you allow tracking (see below), it may also receive your device's advertising identifier.
Firebase Remote Config Tells the app whether a newer version is available or required. A Firebase installation identifier and basic app and device details, such as app version, platform and language.
Firebase In-App Messaging Shows an occasional message on the home screen, such as news about an update. It never appears during a battle. A Firebase installation identifier, and whether a message was shown or tapped.

Google processes this in line with the Google Privacy Policy and Firebase's privacy documentation. We only ever see it in aggregate.

Ads, and what AdMob receives

From version 1.3, 1v1 Battle shows ads from Google AdMob: a banner along the bottom of the home and result screens, and a full-screen ad now and then as you start a battle, before the search for a rival begins. Ads never appear during a battle or while you are looking for a rival, and they are limited to ones suitable for a general audience.

What AdMob may receiveWhy
An identifier for this app on your device, your IP address and a rough location worked out from it, basic device and app details (model, OS version, language, app version), and which ads you saw and tapped To choose, show and count ads, to measure how they perform, and to prevent fraud and abuse
Your device's advertising identifier, only if you allow tracking when your iPhone asks To choose ads using your activity in other companies' apps and websites. Without it, ads are chosen without that history.

After your first battle, the game explains the ads and offers to show your iPhone's tracking question; you can say no, or not now, and the game plays exactly the same. In the UK, the European Economic Area and Switzerland, Google's consent message asks for your choice before any ads load. Google handles this information as described in how Google uses information from apps that use its services and the Google Privacy Policy. We never receive it ourselves, only totals such as how many ads were shown.

What 1v1 Battle does not do

  • No crash reporting, and no tracking across other companies' apps or websites unless you allow it when your iPhone asks.
  • No in-app purchases. The app never asks for payment details.
  • No location services, contacts, photos, camera or microphone.
  • We do not sell your information, and share it only with the services named on this page.

Your choices

  • Change your nickname or champion at any time in your profile.
  • Report a name on the leaderboard with the … button next to it. Their name is hidden on your leaderboard straight away; their place and wins stay as they are.
  • Turn tracking for ads on or off at any time in your iPhone's Settings, under Privacy & Security → Tracking.
  • Change your ad consent in the UK, the EEA and Switzerland with Privacy choices in your profile.
  • Delete everything on your device by uninstalling the app.
  • Ask for your guest account and leaderboard entry to be deleted by opening an issue on the issue tracker. Matchmaking records are deleted automatically within a day either way.

Children

1v1 Battle is meant for a general audience and is not directed at children under 13. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children.

Changes to this policy

If a future version of the app changes how information is handled, this page will be updated before that version is released, and the date below will change.

Contact

Questions about privacy, or a request about your data? Open an issue on the issue tracker and mention 1v1 Battle. It will be answered there.
